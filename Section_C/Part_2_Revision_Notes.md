### 1.3 Highest-priority areas and why

**HIGH — tested or flagged, or a level [2] rule the chapter gets wrong or omits**

1. 🔴 Acceptance of a bill: signature alone, or signature with "accepted" or words of similar import; never the word alone or a date. Tested 🟢 2025–26 Q1 as "which TWO". The chapter (p.59) gives only "signs as accepted" [CORRECTED #9].
2. 🔴 Definite sum (Convention Art 7): a bill remains for a definite sum although payable with interest, by instalments, by instalments with acceleration, according to an exchange rate, or in another currency. Tested 🟢 2025–26 Q2. Absent from the chapter [ADDED: Convention Art 7].
3. 🔴 Sender's payment obligation (Model Law Art 5(6)): the sender becomes obliged to pay the receiving bank when the receiving bank accepts the payment order; payment is due at the start of the execution period. Tested 🟢 2024–25 Q1, "found to be very difficult". Absent from the chapter [ADDED: Model Law Art 5(6)].
4. 🔴 Vocabulary of the credit-transfer chain: originator, sender, receiving bank, intermediary bank, beneficiary's bank, beneficiary. 🟢 Dec 2013: "the majority seemed to think that the sender had to be a bank"; "very few identified the point that originators were also senders". Absent from the chapter [ADDED: Model Law Art 2].
5. 🔴 Authentication of payment orders (Art 5(2)–(4)): who bears an unauthorised order. 🟢 Dec 2016 MCQ; Dec 2013 part (b) "answered less well". Absent from the chapter.
6. 🔴 Transfer of a bill endorsed in blank by mere delivery. 🟢 Dec 2017: poorly done through "unwillingness ... to study that particular area of the syllabus in the required detail". The chapter has the point (p.58) but labels it a "bearer bill" [CORRECTED #4].
7. 🟡 Credit transfer is not a letter of credit. 🟢 Dec 2013: "a fairly common error was to confuse credit transfers with letters of credit". The chapter never mentions credit transfers, so the confusion is live.
8. 🔴 UCP 600: a credit is irrevocable even if silent, and cannot be amended or cancelled without the agreement of the issuing bank, the confirming bank (if any) and the beneficiary. The chapter teaches revocable v irrevocable and amendment "without notifying the seller" [CORRECTED #17, #18]. Level [2].
9. 🔴 The banks' undertakings: issuing bank must honour; confirming bank must honour or negotiate; an advising bank that has not confirmed has no undertaking. The chapter has the advising bank paying and calls undertakings "guarantees" [CORRECTED #13, #15, #16]. Level [2].
10. 🔴 Letters of comfort bind only if their words amount to a promise; 🟢 Kleinwort Benson Ltd v Malaysia Mining Corporation Bhd [1989] 1 WLR 379 (CA). Level [2]; the chapter has the principle without the authority [ADDED: BAILII-hosted judgments citing the case].

**MEDIUM — examinable, not flagged**

- Bills of lading: the three functions, negotiable "to order" v straight, shipped v received for shipment, clean v claused, sea waybill contrast (level [1]).
- Holder and protected holder; guarantee (aval); protest and notice of dishonour; drawer's "without recourse"; four-year limitation; promissory notes.
- Model Law: acceptance and deemed acceptance, execution period, revocation, refund, interest-only liability, completion.

**LOW — keep, lower probability**

- Hague-Visby limits (666.67 units of account per package or 2 per kilogramme, whichever is higher) and one-year time bar; rights of suit under COGSA 1992. Conditional on the examinable documents list (Part 1, open point 6.1).
- Inland, ocean and through bills (chapter p.61); Convention status detail (ten ratifications; not in force).

### 1.4 Reading the question: the examiner's own warnings applied to Section C

- 🟡 Similar words: in Model Law options, underline who pays and who accepts. Both halves of the correct answer name the receiving bank. 🟢 Report 2024–25.
- 🟡 Too-narrow distractors: an option that adds "only" or "must be fixed" to a Convention rule is usually false. Art 7 allows interest, instalments, exchange-rate and foreign-currency terms; Art 8 permits variable reference rates. 🟢 Report 2025–26 Q2.
- 🟡 "Which TWO" with two signature-based answers: both correct options in 2025–26 Q1 involved the drawee's signature; the two wrong options had no signature. Look for the common feature.
- 🟡 Generic v specific: where three specific options each fail for some class of case and one general option is always true, the general option is correct. 🟢 Report 2023–24 Q4 (capital reduction) states the method; it transfers to Section C questions on "which statement is correct".
- 🟡 Unstudied areas: every report warns that skipping hard areas is "risky in the extreme, especially when such topics appear in Section B". For this section the hard areas are the two UNCITRAL texts, which the chapter under-covers.

### 1.6 The three legal systems in Section C

Section C is built on uniform instruments rather than national law, so the common-law, civil-law and Sharia-law comparison is examined here mainly through purpose and origin. 🔴 The Convention was adopted (GA Res 43/165, 9 December 1988) "to overcome the major disparities and uncertainties" between national laws on payment instruments, and applies only where the parties opt in by using the prescribed words [ADDED: UNCITRAL explanatory note]. The chapter and the glossary are silent on any Section C rule that differs between the three systems; a Sharia-law point on interest-bearing instruments would rest on general knowledge alone: SOURCE DOES NOT ESTABLISH THIS (Part 1, open point 6.9). 🟢 Reports 2022–23 and 2023–24: the three systems "will be examined in every exam", so expect them in Sections A and D rather than as Section C detail.
